# Commercially Available Humanoid Robots: Market Snapshot as of October 2026

Research date: 2026-10-16. Many price figures come from resellers or aggregator sites and conflict with each other. Each one is marked with its date and source type. "Vendor claim" means the figure has not been independently verified. Anything dated before 2026 is marked [OLDER].

## 1. Model-by-model comparison: price, payload, hands, battery, autonomy, delivery status

### Takeaway
The only humanoids anyone can actually buy off the shelf in October 2026 come from Chinese vendors: Unitree (R1, G1, H1, H2), AgiBot (X2, A2 Lite), Booster, Fourier and UBTech. They ship internationally. Their payloads are small: about 2–3 kg per arm on the G1 and about 7 kg rated (15 kg peak) per arm on the H2. They are mostly research or education platforms that need developers to program them. The Western industrial humanoids (Figure, Digit, Apollo, Atlas, Optimus) carry 20–50 kg-class loads but are sold only through enterprise pilots or RaaS deals, or not at all.

## 3. Real deployments handling heavy or awkward objects (8–25 kg, tire-like)

### Takeaway
I found no humanoid deployment that handles tires. Production-floor humanoid work in 2026 is light: totes, parts sequencing, sheet-metal placement. Tire automation in the field uses fixed industrial robot arms, not humanoids.

### Cited Findings
- Figure 02 at BMW [2025]: sheet-metal parts handling in the body shop. Figure 03 [Jun 2026]: picking parts from bulk containers into sequence trolleys. No payload was disclosed — [bmwblog](https://www.bmwblog.com/2026/06/26/bmw-figure-03-humanoid-robot-spartanburg-plant/); [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/bmw-figure-03-humanoid-robot-smart-factory-us)
- Digit at GXO and Amazon: moving totes. Digit 5 is rated for about 23 kg repeated lifts (vendor claim) — [siliconcanals](https://siliconcanals.com/?p=109721)
- Tire automation without humanoids: the Automated Tire SmartBay robot does tire service in about 30 minutes versus about 75 for a human, with one technician overseeing up to 3 bays (May 2026) — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/14/ai-robot-tire-change). RoboTire uses industrial arms for tire changes in about 15 minutes, installed at Discount Tire and Creamery Tire — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/tag/robotire/)
- An industry overview notes that in real deployments "simple grippers win; fine hands are lab demos" — [dev.to](https://dev.to/the_daily_flare/humanoid-robots-in-2026-how-close-are-they-to-useful-everyday-work-51kn)

### Inferences
- A 12–25 kg car tire is at or above the rated continuous payload of every humanoid buyable in 2026 (H2 at about 14 kg rated with two arms, Walker S2 at 15 kg). The models that can handle it (Atlas, Digit 5, Optimus) are not for sale to SMBs. For tire handling, a non-humanoid option (cobot, palletizer, tire-change cell) is far more mature in 2026.

### Gaps
- No public trial of a humanoid handling tires or wheels was found.

## 5. Known limitations: reliability, uptime, teleoperation, safety certification

### Takeaway
No humanoid has an independently verified uptime figure or a finalized humanoid-specific safety certification. ISO 25785-1 is still in draft. Battery life is typically 2–4 h. Consumer and home humanoids still rely heavily on teleoperation.

### Cited Findings
- ISO 25785-1 (industrial mobile robots with actively controlled stability, legged ones included) is still under development, with a working draft in May 2025 — [i-scoop](https://www.i-scoop.eu/iso-25785-1-explained-and-what-it-means-for-humanoid-robot-safety/); [theresarobotforthat safety](https://theresarobotforthat.com/?p=672)
- Digit 5 avoids, stops or crouches when people approach, but is not yet cleared for hands-on physical collaboration — [xenospectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/agility-robotics-digit-5-cooperative-safety/)
- Agility's Arc fleet tool tracks uptime and mean time between incidents. No numbers have been published — [techbriefs](https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/53111-safety-in-motion-setting-the-standard-for-humanoid-robots)
- Typical practical ceiling is 3–5 h per charge, and "no current humanoid works a full shift untethered" without swaps or charging — [dev.to](https://dev.to/the_daily_flare/humanoid-robots-in-2026-how-close-are-they-to-useful-everyday-work-51kn). Mitigations: Walker S2 hot-swaps its battery in about 3 minutes, Atlas has hot-swappable packs, and Figure 03 has wireless charging (sources above).
- Teleoperation: NEO is about 60–70% autonomous, with Expert Mode remote control for the rest — [ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/1x-neo-ships-into-homes-with-a-human-teleoperator-still-inside)
- Optimus V3 units have not yet met the durability and reliability thresholds for external leasing (Sep 2026 report) — [tmcnet](https://blog.tmcnet.com/blog/rich-tehrani/robot/tesla-confirms-optimus-robot-will-ship-with-a-new-design.html)
- Apptronik publishes little operating data, with no uptime or throughput figures — [newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/humanoid-robotics-apptronik-upate)
- Unitree G1 warranty is only 8 months (18 months on the EDU), a reliability signal for a consumer-grade platform — [Unitree](https://www.unitree.com/g1/)

### Inferences
- Off-the-shelf Chinese humanoids (Unitree, Booster, AgiBot X2) arrive with locomotion and demo behaviors but no trained industrial task skills. Productive work requires in-house development (SDK, teleoperation data collection, VLA training), which most SMBs do not have.

### Gaps
- No independent MTBF or uptime figures for any humanoid. No CE or NR-12 (Brazil machinery safety norm) compliance information was found for any humanoid.
